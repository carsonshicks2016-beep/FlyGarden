# Completed odor-only causal evaluation

All 60 registered neural trials (20 matched seeds × three conditions) completed. Independent source, root ordering, input RNG, spike-count, timing and motor-decoder audits passed. All 20 native motor replays reproduce complete recorded body/gait/world state and poses exactly without neural recomputation.

## Result

Odor causes a reproducible neural motor-command change relative to sensory-disabled/support-only and targeted DNa02 interruption controls. Useful directional orientation fails: only 10/20 trials exceed the registered 0.05-radian toward-heading improvement; 16/20 was required. All 20 exceed 0.1-mm departure and none flip. Directional paired confidence intervals cross zero for both controls. The candidate is not ready for progression to food/navigation/learning.

The mean toward-heading effect relative to sensory-off is -0.0030 rad (97.5% interval -0.1080 to 0.0987); relative to steering interruption it is 0.0152 rad (-0.0982 to 0.1228). Motor RMS differences are 0.2064 (0.2005–0.2125) and 0.1748 (0.1704–0.1795). These are compensated-sum, cue-stratified 10,000-resample paired estimates with two-comparison correction.

## Boundaries

This is a 3-second engineered odor-only fixture using the full modeled network, fixed 65-Hz locomotor support, supplied gait and fixed decoder, with vision input disabled and learning frozen. The support-only condition is the sensory-off condition, not an additional independent group. Conditions receive local sensory observations from their own physical trajectories. Twenty stochastic seeds are not twenty biological animals. No navigation, learning, vision or consciousness claim follows. Raw results and all failed directional trials are preserved.
