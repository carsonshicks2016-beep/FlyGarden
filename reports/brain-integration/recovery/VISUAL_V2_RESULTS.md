# Versioned visual encoding repair

The original encoder emits an expansion impulse on sudden appearance and responds to sideways translation. It is preserved unchanged with its previous experiments.

The new eye-continuous-expansion-v2 encoder requires an already-present dark component, growth on both image axes, and two consecutive expanding intervals. It takes only eye images and elapsed simulated time. Uniform LPLC2 drive remains an engineered mapping; neither natural receptive fields nor physiological equivalence are claimed.

Archived native-image screening: blank, static-left/right and translating-pattern streams produced zero rates. Both looming sides retained ipsilateral activity, and wall approach retained bilateral activity. These archived results are retrospective screening.

A separate prospective native fixture set used new object distances/angles and25ms sampling. Blank, sudden static appearance, translation and occlusion all remained silent. Left/right looming produced15/13 active frames with the correct side at up to100Hz. The two short self-motion fixtures remained silent. All registered bounded gates passed. Complete raw eye images, feature streams, source snapshots and hashes are retained.

This does not establish general self-motion disambiguation: retinal expansion caused by approaching a stationary obstacle can resemble external looming. No general obstacle navigation or sensory-dependent brain choice is established. The revised encoder has not yet replaced the public application's controller or been integrated into the saved live-scene loop.

Next: integrate this version through a distinct sensory adapter and repeat checkpoint continuation, then commit held-out causal behavior protocols without changing the frozen neural decoder or learning rules.
