# Requirements remaining after this anatomical audit

This stage acquires exact site coordinates and tests a published anatomical registration. It neither changes the controller nor proves that a glomerulus is an electrical compartment.

1. **Opposite-side anatomy:** the frozen published FlyWire mirror fails the predeclared glomerular label gate. Seek independently defined opposite-side glomerular boundaries, or a separately registered local refinement with calibration anchors separated from held-out validation. Do not reuse a failed global mirror, silently translate meshes until they fit, infer an entire patchy arbor from a partner label, or rename split glomeruli without a source. The tested mirror's failure also applies to DM1.
2. **Branch topology:** join exact version-783 root skeletons to the distinct release/reception points. Record missing morphology and branch-distance uncertainty. Glomerular mesh enclosure alone does not establish electrotonic isolation, electrical coupling strength, or local transmitter release.
3. **Subtype and physiology:** retain the qualified annotation bridge for lLN2P_a/b/c. The authors' numerical reference describes LN2P_c and was fitted to DC3. That fit is not a physiological fit for the present DM1/lLN2P_b pathway. Establish incoming-spike to local-activity units, inhibitory targets, receptor-specific action and release/decay assumptions explicitly. Preserve other inputs and mutual local-neuron connections.
4. **Separate candidate:** after anatomical and physiological choices are reviewable, freeze a new full-network protocol with every omitted or altered connection manifested. Preserve the original brain, graph, checkpoints and existing failed runs. Do not import unmodeled archive partners into the controller automatically.
5. **Validation:** validate the adapter against the published isolated reference, timestep convergence, and complete-state continuation first. Then repeat the unchanged full-network response, recovery and contrast gates. Only a passing sensory response candidate advances to causal body-direction/navigation and later controlled learning evaluation.
