# Small discriminating checks before another expensive sweep

2026-10-08. **Proposed experiments, not registered or executed.** Exact roots, measured stimulus values, solver parameters, seeds and acceptance bounds must be frozen in each protocol after the corresponding source data and numerical fixtures are inspected. Do not invent numbers now and call them biological validation.

## Priority 1: import identity and complete-graph audit

This is data validation, not neural simulation. Register the exact archived v2 sources and a streamed processing path. Verify full checksums, release-key joins, unique identities, neuronal membership, duplicate/self edges, count positivity and site-total reconciliation. Resolve simple-table autapse omissions and unknown endpoints before a full-graph claim. Reproduce a small named route's edge counts with an independent reader.

Discriminates: importer/version mistakes versus physiological failures. Stop before simulation on unresolved mismatched keys, unsupported membership decisions or unaccounted omissions. A decoder cannot repair a wrong anatomical join.

## Priority 2: measured front-leg sensory and graded-response fixture

Retrieve selected physiology files and check time, angle, voltage/calcium units, stimulus schedules, sex/strain, behavioral/tethering state, driver metadata and license. Establish exact-root or explicitly class-level correspondence; the latter cannot support an exact-cell claim. Begin with imposed femur–tibia position/ramp movements and claw/13B-alpha candidates where the mapping supports them.

Compare a documented graded response family with the existing common spiking default on the **same measured input**. Hold anatomy and all other hypotheses fixed. Measure steady-state tuning, onset/offset timing, hysteresis, voltage versus angle, spiking where observed, and recovery. Include an explicit measurement model when comparing voltage to fluorescence; do not equate raw calcium values with spike rate.

Discriminates: wrong cell model versus wrong sensory transduction/mapping. First use directly imposed sensory traces to isolate the receiving cell; then use body-to-organ encoding. Failure under imposed traces points downstream; failure only after organ encoding points to the adapter. Missing correspondence stops that specific claim rather than producing arbitrary neuron indices.

## Priority 3: synaptic-effect and body-force fixtures

For a matched sensory/interneuron/motor path, inspect transmitter/co-transmitter evidence and postsynaptic-effect assumptions. Use unitary events or continuous presynaptic activity to check recipient sign, scale, time constants, reset/refractory behavior and delay. Separate fast conductance, graded release and slow modulation. If electrical coupling is plausible but not measured in the graph, document it as an unresolved model component.

Separately activate a named motor target on the supported body and verify joint/force direction, activation decay and antagonistic mechanics. This is a physical adapter check, not evidence of nervous-system control.

Discriminates: neural-effect errors versus reversed muscle/joint conventions. Do not fit a desired navigation turn to mask either failure.

## Priority 4: closed leg-feedback and full-network repeat

Close the qualified local loop: imposed perturbation → organ response → reconstructed delivery → motor activation → body movement → changed organ input. Compare intact feedback with matched sensory-frozen and motor-disconnected conditions; keep the body, stimulus and initial state identical. Measure sign/latency of corrective motion, settling, stability and work/force bounds. A tethered one-joint result does not qualify free walking.

Repeat the same sensory response in the full supported graph with no new fitting. Record incoming delivery from the rest of the network, saturation and the state of every relevant graded/spiking unit. Preserve failures rather than cutting recurrent inputs until a curve looks right.

Discriminates: validated local mechanism versus a full-network operating-state mismatch. Register a single-worker memory/runtime gate before extended repeats. No new parameter grid follows automatically from a failure.

## Priority 5: native steering and sensory-context checks

After a stable full-network/body operating state, use published steering stimuli and native descending-to-cord-to-motor outputs. Test both sides, no stimulus, bilateral and unequal conditions when justified by that stimulus's physiology. Use the actual body turn, matched output-disconnection/intervention controls and unrewarded sensor-only probes; preserve direction, latency and stimulus identity in recordings.

A local odor gradient is a later sensory/behavior test, not a reason to require every interneuron pair to be antisymmetric. New route-appropriate metrics must be introduced prospectively. They do not convert any historical failed PN/DNa gate into a pass. The existing garden's useful direction and controlled-learning goals remain unresolved until measured in the body.

## Decision rules common to all checks

- Register one discriminating hypothesis at a time; keep calibration and held-out validation separated. Do not combine adaptation, sign changes, new input encoders and motor remapping in one diagnostic.
- Exact source hashes, equations, units, neuron order, mappings, stimulus schedules, solver timing, seeds, stop rules and metric windows are required before execution.
- Numerical continuation and recording parity are operational requirements; they do not establish physiological accuracy.
- Unknown or unavailable measurements produce an explicit limitation, not an invented parameter or a successful badge.
- Preserve old sources, evidence, checkpoint identities and all 39 requirement texts/statuses. Fixing an importer/numerical bug and changing a physiological hypothesis are separately recorded actions.
- No PPO/GA, native-route promotion, learning fit or bulk simulation sweep is part of this proposal.
