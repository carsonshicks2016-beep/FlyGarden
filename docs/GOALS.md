# Fly Garden: agreed biological-fidelity goals

Decision ID: biological-fidelity-goals-v1. Agreed with Carson on 2026-10-07.
Recorded on 2026-10-08.
Status: approved project direction; the proposed anatomy and body platforms still require feasibility checks. This document records goals, not achieved capabilities or an executable experiment protocol.

## Central objective

Build a persistent virtual fruit fly whose sensory processing, internal neural dynamics, action selection, and eventual lower-level motor control follow biological evidence as closely as available data and validated models allow. The modeled nervous system should receive signals generated by its simulated senses and body, and produce behavior through reconstructed neural pathways.

Biological fidelity takes priority over immediate gameplay competence or real-time performance. The intended destination remains Fly Garden: one fly exploring a 3D environment, finding and consuming food, negotiating obstacles, avoiding threats, and changing its choices through biologically grounded modeled plasticity.

This is an open-ended research objective. A connectome does not supply the original animal's complete physiology or functional state. More detail, more active neurons, a successful video, or a working game does not by itself establish greater biological fidelity.

## Proposed anatomical and physical foundations

- **BANC is the first-choice anatomical foundation to assess.** It reconstructs a female fly's connected brain and ventral nerve cord in one specimen, allowing sensory-to-motor pathways to be traced across the neck. Importing its anatomy does not automatically provide working physiology or behavior.
- **MaleCNS is the comparison and alternative candidate.** Compare actual downloadable coverage, annotations, known defects, licenses, access, circuit mappings, and runtime before committing to migration. A change of preferred foundation must have a documented reason.
- **Retain the existing FlyWire v783 model and all its evidence.** It remains a reference and diagnostic model. BANC, MaleCNS, and FlyWire contain different specimens and identifiers; their neurons, morphology, learned weights, and full checkpoints are not interchangeable. Any cross-dataset reconstruction must explicitly identify its source, cell-type matching, uncertainty, and introduced assumptions.
- **Use the existing walking body as the initial test platform.** Supplied leg coordination remains an identified temporary scaffold and comparison, not proof of reconstructed nerve-cord control.
- **Evaluate FlyBody for later walking/flight mechanics.** Its physical flight support and published trained controllers do not establish that our reconstructed nervous system can fly. Its approximate aerodynamics must remain documented; it is not a claim of fully resolved airflow.

BANC and MaleCNS omit parts of early vision and contain other reconstruction defects. Missing pathways must be identified before use; any completion from other datasets or physiological models must be labeled. Recording this decision performs no migration or bulk download.

## Required sensory-to-action architecture

The intended closed loop is environment -> modeled sensory organs -> brain and nerve cord -> motor outputs and body mechanics -> new sensory feedback.

- Vision begins with light sampled through the simulated eyes, followed by documented photoreceptor and visual-circuit response models. Validate retinal geometry, temporal responses, own-body masking, occlusion, and motion/approach responses.
- Smell begins with chemical exposure at actual modeled antennal locations, followed by receptor-specific response models where supported. Retain the current two synthetic channels and distance-based fields as identified comparisons; improve them against biological observations rather than silently calling them natural odors or fluid plumes.
- Touch, taste, limb position, and body movement begin with physical contact and body observations. Map these to identified sensory populations, keeping missing mappings explicit.
- The running neural controller receives no food or predator coordinates, arena map, target route, cue-side labels, or game-score instructions. World geometry can generate sensory input and drive the engineered predator; it cannot directly select the fly's actions.
- Electrical-unit models, synaptic transmission, receptor effects, adaptation, neuromodulation, and plasticity should reflect supported cell/circuit differences. Add complexity only with a testable purpose and evidence. Do not assume every visual neuron spikes or that one global neuron model fits every population.
- Calibration estimates uncertain biological parameters against neural or behavioral observations, with separate validation. Calibration of a model, fitting an external readout, and experience-dependent learning remain distinct.
- The target is behavior generated through relevant neural pathways into motor circuitry. Adapters for units and physical actuation remain necessary and visible; they must not silently replace decision circuitry with a navigator.

The proposed PN-to-turn engineered readout is no longer the primary development path. Keep it, existing supplied controllers, tonic walking drive, and supplied escape behavior as explicitly labeled diagnostic comparisons or temporary scaffolds. Their success cannot qualify the biological controller. The existing live application remains an engineered hybrid until a separately validated replacement exists.

## Reconnecting the nervous system to the garden

Proceed through evidence gates, retaining failed results:

1. Establish local sensory/body feedback and reproducible neural responses before goal-directed tasks.
2. Validate stopping, turning, and stable walking through the chosen action pathway, including contact, collisions, falls, and stalls.
3. Add food-derived odor exposure and actual taste/contact signals; test approach and feeding-related responses without privileged coordinates.
4. Validate motion and looming responses with controlled visual stimuli; then make an arena predator produce those inputs through its movement and visibility.
5. Combine food, obstacles, shelter, and danger on unfamiliar layouts. Observe food acquisition, captures, escapes, travel, falls, and stalls rather than inserting hidden rules to guarantee success.
6. Introduce supported internal-state and reinforcement mechanisms. Show a causal path from the selected plastic connections to behavior before testing acquisition, retention, reversal, and generalization.

The observer may score food and survival, but the score is not a substitute neural reinforcement input. Energy remains a simplified game variable until a separately validated physiological model exists. Immediate escape responses and learned avoidance must be reported separately.

Preserve Sandbox, Challenges, and Experiments; arena editing; checkpoints and lineage; neuron-level anatomical viewing; synchronized recordings; free-camera playback; and automatic MP4 export as the eventual application experience. Adapt these interfaces to the selected model's signals, including continuous activity for non-spiking cells where applicable. A saved modeled state does not establish preserved consciousness or the original animal's identity.

## Flight is a later goal

Design interfaces to accommodate flight, but validate walking and sensory control first. Later qualify wing actuation and force generation, takeoff, stabilization, steering, and landing as separate checks. Closing flight sensory feedback and demonstrating neural control are additional requirements beyond a flying physical body or a trained flight policy.

Elevated food, aerial escape, and landing sites are eventual garden activities. Our present Fly Garden walking controller does not fly. A realistic-looking flight animation or supplied flight controller cannot qualify biological flight control.

## Evidence, persistence, and compute boundaries

- Preserve all historical protocols, raw recordings, failures, checkpoints, and their model identities. The existing 39 acceptance requirements and qualification statuses are unchanged by this goals decision. Register any new scientific protocol or changed criterion prospectively; do not relabel old failures.
- Retain the original controlled-learning requirements, including at least 20 independent matched seeded states per condition, frozen and shuffled controls, unrewarded probes, and the required 95% bootstrap and reversal checks. Any future amendment must be an explicit scientific change.
- Aim to run the full supported modeled nervous system. Smaller circuit fixtures may diagnose mechanisms; they do not substitute for a full-network qualification. A reduced interactive product mode requires a separate scope decision.
- Share simulation time across neural and physical dynamics. Slow computation is acceptable: compute at its natural speed and use recorded playback without skipping dynamics to meet wall time.
- Benchmark actual memory, runtime, and storage before long runs. Preserve the 2 GiB free-space reserve and never automatically delete research evidence. Record interruptions and resume from verified state.
- Full checkpoints preserve all supported transient neural, body, world, adaptation/learning, decoder, and random-generator state. Recordings and MP4s are viewing artifacts, not mechanisms for resuming a brain.
- No completion date or guarantee of a fully faithful digital animal is established. Report anatomical coverage, physiological assumptions, causal validation, useful behavior, learning, and operational readiness separately.

## Immediate next work

Conduct a read-only BANC/MaleCNS anatomy-and-physiology feasibility assessment: verify downloadable data and provenance; inventory sensory, ascending, descending, and motor pathways and missing regions; identify published response measurements for initial validation; estimate resource needs; and propose a versioned architecture and the smallest discriminating experiments.

Implementation, migration, and scientific runs require concrete designs and registered protocols before execution. This goals log does not launch simulations, fit a controller, change production behavior, or reinterpret earlier results.
